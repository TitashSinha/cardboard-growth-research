EX01 is a small descriptive hypothesis test, not a randomized growth-lift experiment. Its initial buyer-research section informs the intervention: a prototype that helps decide whether to keep or investigate a workflow. Neither section proves purchase intent.

## Audience and eligibility

Main cohort: people doing marketing in a self-reported small SaaS team (operational definition: 1–10 people doing marketing/content), who personally produced/edited a completed feature/launch video in the last 90 days and influence tool/workflow choice. Include satisfied incumbents; do not recruit only complaints. Record role, project date and platform initially. Ask named Cardboard familiarity only after neutral behavior questions and before aid exposure, then finalize stratum. Exclude competing-tool sellers from main cohort; record a separate context stratum if useful. Commissioning-only and existing Cardboard-user accounts are context conversations, not pooled main-cohort observations.

Aim for the first five eligible, consented main-cohort conversations; not a representative sample. The sourcing file has five unverified leads, including one separate historical familiarity lead, so it does not guarantee five eligible participants. Recruitment needs route-specific authorization. Permission to retain de-identified summaries in the private repository/work sample is separate from taking notes; excerpt consent is separate again. No screen recording or private customer media is required. Do not commit a P-ID-to-public-lead/source mapping. Retain consent version and withdrawal status without identities; remove retained participant material if consent is withdrawn. Report withdrawals, do not hide them or add replacements merely to reach a threshold.

## Hypothesis and materials

H03: current methods may already meet the task well enough that switching has no established reason. Alternative: multiple recent projects reveal a concrete material unresolved correction/handoff problem worth investigating.

Materials: [neutral guide](../../research/buyer-research-guide.md); [decision aid v1.1](materials/decision-aid-v1.1.md); [results.csv](results.csv); [case template](case-template.md). Initial [v1](materials/decision-aid-v1.md) is preserved; v1.1 adds a neutral “none” option after internal review. Freeze v1.1 once collection begins. The aid is an independent prototype, not Cardboard UI, footage or tested output.

## Sequence and neutral tasks

1. Screen and obtain consent; assign P ID. Keep private mapping outside Git.
2. Ask about the last completed project before naming Cardboard/showing the aid. Map steps/tools, what worked, corrections, consequences, spending and actual keep/replace decisions. Ask for concrete examples, not hypothetical willingness to pay. Participant estimates remain labeled estimates.
3. Classify the primary behavior evidence using the criteria below, preserving uncertainty. Do not infer dissatisfaction from lengthy projects alone.
4. Ask named Cardboard familiarity, finalize stratum, then show v1.1. Ask: “Use this sheet to summarize that project. Which next step follows from your experience? What is missing or hard to understand?” Participant fills or explains the route; record resulting decision and feedback, including rejection of the aid.
5. Restate factual notes for participant correction. Obtain separate excerpt permission. No signup request or sales pitch.

All main participants receive this order. There is no paired-version or comparative-effect estimate. The prior interview may prime the aid task; record this limitation. Record prior Cardboard knowledge before exposure. If a later offer A/B task is run, preregister assignment/order and never combine it with EX01 outcomes retrospectively.

## Primary observable classification

Denominator: the first five eligible, consented main-cohort cases, including mixed/insufficient accounts. Do not replace cases because they are ambiguous or inconvenient. Report screened, eligible, classified/mixed, excluded and withdrawn counts separately. If consent withdrawal prevents using a case, report the reduced analyzable count and do not apply the five-case rule.

For both live and written modes, reserve places in screening-plus-retention-consent order under the amendment. No-answer nonresponses remain missing reserved places, not feedback rows or classified cases. Report partial/missing cases and mode separately; do not apply the five-case rule with fewer than five analyzable reserved cases. Public recommendation replies are adjacent evidence and cannot enter this denominator.

- **Problem-positive:** names a concrete correction/handoff failure on that completed project; describes a material consequence in their own terms (e.g. additional rework, missed deadline, blocked approval); explains why the present workflow leaves it unresolved. A delivered video can qualify if the participant describes recurring material rework as an unresolved workflow burden, with a concrete example. Record the finished correction and unresolved burden separately. Link each criterion to a note/excerpt; record repeat-task evidence separately. A vague “editing takes time” is insufficient.
- **Adequate incumbent:** says the delivered result met the task and gives a concrete reason for retaining the workflow; any correction was acceptably resolved and the participant does not identify a material unresolved workflow burden. Do not interpret unknowns as adequacy.
- **Mixed/unclear:** account is contradictory or insufficient to decide whether the resolution/burden was acceptable, or concerns are only hypothetical. Keep the reason; do not force a binary label.

Secondary asset observations: could the participant map input, correction, consequence and incumbent strength; which branch they chose; confusing/missing fields; whether the aid suggested evaluating a tool despite an adequate workflow. No numeric “quality score.”

## Directional rule, specified before collection

At the first five eligible, consented main-cohort cases (mixed cases stay in denominator):

- If at least three are adequate-incumbent, deprioritize a broad switch-to-Cardboard claim. Revise the asset around retaining the current method or a narrower exceptional task.
- If at least three are problem-positive and at least two independently name the same unresolved task, investigate that task with a proof/message test. This is a reason to investigate, not evidence Cardboard solves it.
- Otherwise, narrow the question using contradictions or collect a small targeted follow-up only if authorized; do not manufacture a winner.

Fewer than five analyzable cases: report counts/context and individual learnings; no threshold verdict. Thresholds are practical investigation rules, not estimated market majorities. Known users and vendor-affiliated accounts remain separate. A nonresponse/decline is not product rejection. If the aid pushes inappropriate switching in two cases, revise its wording before reusing it, while leaving original v1/v1.1 intact.

## Failure and stop conditions

Do not collect without authorization/consent. Stop a session if privacy or consent is unclear. Exclude ineligible cases with reason; do not pool them. If most leads cannot describe a recent completed task, revise sourcing rather than declaring no demand. If Cardboard use is required to answer a question, mark it unanswerable under D003. If evidence contradicts the working segment, reopen D008.

Record no results until actual responses arrive. After analysis, create v2 and a [revision record](revision-log.md) linking changes to consented, nonidentifying P IDs/contradictions; update H03/H04 and later briefs. Internal review is not buyer feedback. Completed-project eligibility creates survivorship bias: abandoned/unshipped work is outside this test, so results cannot describe the whole production journey. No significance, conversion, revenue, product-performance or willingness-to-pay claims.
