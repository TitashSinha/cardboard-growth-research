## Proposed page structure

The following is a content specification, not final reader-facing copy. Editorial labels stay outside the eventual page body except the visible label on an illustrative example.

1. **Hero:** working headline, “Build a first cut from your SaaS demo recording.” Explain that Cardboard describes editable first cuts and refinement by chat or timeline. Avoid automatic interface fidelity, finished-video quality or time-saving promises. Primary navigation CTA: “See the workflow example,” linking to the on-page illustrative section, never a nonexistent video.
2. **Concrete task:** one fictional product recording, one feature explanation and one narration track. State the desired sequence and review criteria. Do not invent client names, a completed project or participant experience.
3. **Input → request → first cut → correction → export:** an explicitly illustrative storyboard showing what a real evaluation would inspect. Mark first-cut and corrected-output panels as evidence required, not completed Cardboard output. Use textual panels, not fabricated application screenshots.
4. **What the proof must show:** recording/asset provenance, exact editing request, genuine output, editability, changes made and remaining manual work. Use existing lawful company samples first if they meet these requirements; their suitability is currently unverified.
5. **Fit and boundaries:** distinguish rough assembly from exact animation/control needs. If the existing editor meets the task, retaining it is a reasonable choice. Do not claim Cardboard replaces CapCut, After Effects or a whole production workflow. NLE export is a listed paid-plan capability; round-trip fidelity and supported transfer details require verification.
6. **Access and plans:** direct readers to current pricing and platform information. Separate browser from Mac access. Do not duplicate the historical price ladder or promise a universal free trial. Confirm current device, account, agent-subscription, generation and export requirements before any real evaluation.
7. **Short FAQ:** existing footage versus generated assets; how to assess corrections; handoff requirements; where to check access. Keep answers specific and distinguish documented capability from an untested requirement.
8. **Final next action:** “Check plans and access,” linking to official pricing with a separate Mac-information link. No purchase recommendation, guaranteed outcome or invented product demonstration. A future “View the actual demo and corrections” CTA is gated on a real proof package and a verified destination.

## Illustrative storyboard requirements

Use a fictional dashboard feature: choose a filter and inspect the changed result. A proposed request asks for a concise explanation using the supplied recording, without inventing interface states. Review whether labels remain legible, actions stay in sequence and narration matches the displayed action. A hypothetical correction asks for more time on the result screen.

This tests the communication design only. It does not assert that Cardboard successfully preserves the interface, follows the request, supports a particular correction or exports a faithful project. No durations, edit counts or savings are claimed as measured results.
