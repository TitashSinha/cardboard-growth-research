## Evidence rules

Every material claim needs a source URL, access date, scope, and evidence ID. Label it verified, observed, inferred, proposed, or untested. Log failed access and coverage limits.

Separate observed search positions from estimated tool data. Record country, query, date, device/session context, and organic versus sponsored results. Search API output is not a substitute for a Google rank observation.

For ChatGPT tests, record model if visible, search on/off, exact prompt, date, fresh conversation, mention, recommendation, citation, citation URL, and ordering. A mention share across sampled prompts is not a market-wide ranking. Never fabricate ChatGPT answers through a different model or tool. If direct testing is unavailable, prepare a manual test sheet and continue other work.

Do not infer a company-wide failure from one search. Do not describe a page as absent without checking navigation, sitemap, site search, and redirects where accessible. Treat hidden analytics and funnel performance as unknown.

Without paid keyword tools, collect demand signals from observed SERPs, autocomplete, related searches, question patterns, public discussions, and comparison pages. Google Trends can show relative interest when data exists, not absolute volume. These signals support hypotheses, not traffic forecasts. Record the collection method; do not automate Google scraping in ways that violate its access controls.

An old-name LinkedIn URL, a search snippet, or a review is evidence only for what it actually shows. Prefer primary product documentation for capabilities. Use public reviews and discussions to discover objections, then corroborate where possible; do not assume reviewers represent Cardboard's customers.

## Growth leadership decision framework

The central decision is: Which buyer, facing which job and trigger, could choose Cardboard over their current workflow, and what evidence and distribution would make that choice easier?

Awareness is the first funnel stage, but more exposure is not automatically the best first intervention. Map the broad market, prioritize commercially relevant searches, and connect every acquisition idea to activation and payment. The project must support the following decisions before content production:

1. Segment: compare marketing teams, agencies, and professional creators. Evaluate recurring task frequency, urgency, existing spend/workflow, product fit, discoverability, and evidence of willingness to pay. Unknowns remain explicit.
2. Positioning: specify the current alternative, meaningful differentiation, proof, and switching friction. Competitors include manual editing, existing suites, outsourced editors, and doing nothing—not just other AI apps.
3. Motion: assess self-serve, team-led adoption, and sales-assisted evaluation against the public pricing and product experience. Do not recommend demos for everyone or enterprise outreach without evidence.
4. Channel: compare search/content, AI discovery, creator demonstrations, partnerships, and relevant communities. Select one primary channel and one supporting channel with a reason to expect qualified users. No paid media spend in this project.
5. Value and economics: document public price, proposed buyer value, workflow frequency, and likely support/compute concerns. CAC, LTV, gross margin, churn, and conversion are unknown without internal data. Optional break-even scenarios need explicit assumptions and sensitivity ranges; they are not company metrics.
6. Experiment: choose the smallest test that could challenge the leading hypothesis. Set a decision rule before interpreting outcomes. Reject ideas that only produce more content without testing a buyer or distribution assumption.

Maintain strategy/decision-log.md with decision, evidence, counterevidence, confidence, alternative rejected, and what would change the decision. Rank opportunities qualitatively by commercial relevance, evidence strength, expected mechanism, and effort. Do not invent precise ICE/RICE scores from unknown reach or conversion.

## Phase 3 — AAARRR strategy and measurement

Dependency: Phase 2. Draft the conversion page and measurement design concurrently.

| Stage | Proposed outcome | Candidate measurement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Awareness | Suitable people encounter Cardboard | Relevant search impressions; sampled AI mention/citation share; qualified distribution reach |
| Acquisition | They visit the product or relevant page | Organic/referral visits; query and page CTR where available |
| Activation | They experience a useful first result | First usable export/share; time to that event |
| Retention | They repeat the workflow | Second project/export within a defined period; cohort return rate |
| Revenue | They pay for continued value | Activated-to-paid conversion; revenue per paying account |
| Referral | They introduce others | Teammate invitations; attributable referred activations |

Activation events are hypotheses until validated. Define event properties and denominators, anonymous versus account identity, consent-aware attribution, and account-level versus individual measures. Do not equate raw reach with awareness or an export with useful output without qualification.

Build an experiment backlog for all stages. Each experiment needs audience, hypothesis, change, baseline availability, primary metric, guardrail, minimum interpretable sample or directional learning rule, dependencies, effort, and stop/continue criteria. When traffic is unknown, do not promise statistical significance or fixed results in a fixed week.

Split the backlog into independently executable checks and company-dependent experiments. Prioritize at most three initial experiments; map later stages without pretending to test retention or revenue externally. Independent checks can include page/message evaluations against the same rubric, repeated discovery observations, and a lawful product test if free access exists. Audience feedback needs actual participants; until recruited, provide the script and mark it unrun. Changes to search samples are monitoring observations, not attribution to unpublished drafts.

For each primary channel, prepare a distribution package: target audience/location, format, message, example draft, link destination, and expected next action. No posting or unsolicited messages without authorization. Include one example of how a product demonstration supports an article or comparison page. A plan for distribution is required even though the samples remain unpublished.

Draft a use-case page and demo storyboard: input → requested edits → finished result → limitations → next action. Use measured time savings only if actually tested. Suggest a hybrid self-serve/team CTA only when supported by the product.

Done: Full funnel, event specification, conversion-page draft, demo storyboard, and prioritized experiments. Live implementation is explicitly separate.
