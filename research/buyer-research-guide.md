# Buyer research guide — recent behavior first

2026-10-03. Prepared, not used. Start from [public evidence classification](buyer-evidence-review.md) and [EX01 protocol](../experiments/workflow-fit/protocol.md); public posts do not establish eligibility or consent.

Live or written participation follows the precollection [v1.2 amendment](../experiments/workflow-fit/async-amendment.md). Written: consent/screen first; questions 1–5 then 6–10; behavior notes before named familiarity/aid; factual readback and excerpt permission last. Public recommendation comments are a separate evidence stream, not consented EX01 responses.

## Screen and consent

“I’m preparing an independent growth work sample about SaaS video workflows. This is voluntary and unpaid. May I take anonymized notes? Separately, may I retain a de-identified summary in my private GitHub repository and hiring work sample? I’ll ask separately about any short excerpt. You can skip questions, stop, or withdraw permission for retained material. Please avoid private customer data.” Record permission for each scope; no retained summary without retention consent.

Confirm: personally produced/edited a completed SaaS feature/launch video within 90 days; approximate date; role and marketing/content-team size; influence on workflow/tool choice; available desktop/platform; any affiliation with a competing tool. Ask named Cardboard familiarity after neutral behavior questions, before showing the aid; finalize stratum then. If not eligible, ask permission for a separate context conversation, never pool it. No recording by default. Private consent/identity records and P-ID-to-public-lead mapping stay outside Git.

## Neutral questions — about 15–20 minutes

1. Tell me about the last product video you finished. What was it for, when was it due, where did it go and what did you deliver?
2. Walk me through what you actually did from source material to delivery. Which tools and people were involved?
3. What worked well enough that you would keep it? What was already familiar or paid for?
4. Tell me about a correction you actually made. What was wrong, how did you notice it and how did you fix it? If none, record that.
5. What consequence did that correction have? Ask for specifics only after their account. If they estimate time, label it estimated; no invented precision.
6. Who approved the video? How did feedback arrive, and what did you hand off? What had to remain editable?
7. What did you spend on that project or existing tools, including zero? Separate recurring subscription, freelancer and already-owned tools; declining to answer is valid.
8. Did you consider or try another method for that project? What did you actually try, keep, replace or abandon—and why?
9. Has that same task/problem occurred before? Ask for another actual example; no assumption of weekly frequency.
10. What remains unresolved today? What would count as an acceptable result for this specific task?

Do not lead with “Would you use Cardboard?” or hypothetical payment questions. Do not equate time spent with wasted time. Probe contradictions politely: “You said the result met the deadline, but approval was difficult. How did those fit together?”

## Decision-aid task and closing

After the behavior questions, ask prior Cardboard familiarity and show [v1.1](../experiments/workflow-fit/materials/decision-aid-v1.1.md). Ask the participant to map their actual project, choose the route and point out unclear/missing parts. Ask what, if anything, the sheet gets wrong. Do not steer toward switching or supply a fictional Cardboard result.

Read back key factual notes for correction. Ask separate permission for each short anonymized excerpt to be retained in the private repository/work sample; record scope. Declined quote consent means paraphrase only if anonymized-note consent permits it, otherwise no retained participant data. Thank them; no tool signup or follow-up message without authorization.
