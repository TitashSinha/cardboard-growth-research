## Required corrections

### 1. Article 3, line 50 — Feedback sharing does not establish editable project access

The native-project-sharing bullet places Cardboard's team-feedback link beside a definition of opening the original editable structure. This invites a capability inference the cited page does not establish. The [Cardboard homepage](https://www.cardboard.ai/) describes link sharing for feedback; it does not establish recipient editing rights in that passage. The submitted editor checklist acknowledges the uncertainty, but the reader-facing text needs it too.

Suggested replacement:

> **Native project sharing.** A project that the recipient can open and edit in its original structure. Confirm their editing permissions and access requirements. Cardboard describes sharing a link for team feedback; that description alone does not establish editable project access for the recipient.

Keep feedback/review links and editable handoffs separate. Update the matching checklist row.

### 2. Article 3, line 28 — “Any change” to a flattened video is too absolute

The sentence says any change requires regeneration or rebuilding. Rendered footage can still be trimmed, reframed or combined with new elements, as the same article later acknowledges. The constraint concerns recovering and independently changing the original embedded elements.

Suggested replacement:

> If a caption, graphic or animation is baked into the video, you cannot adjust its original settings separately. You may need the source project, a regenerated asset or a rebuilt section, depending on the correction.

### 3. Article 5, line 63 — Missing verified evidence is not proof nobody described a task

The note says no respondent described adapting a delivered demo for social. The supplied brief established no verified respondent adaptation case; it did not provide a complete audited negative census. The [closeout](../experiments/workflow-fit/collection-closeout-2026-10-06.md) retains incomplete screening and collection limits.

Replace that clause with:

> The retained evidence does not establish a verified respondent case of adapting a delivered demo for social.

Do not change this into a finding that makers never repurpose demos. Keep the illustrative workflow label.

## Small editorial corrections

- **Article 2, line 12:** prioritize the exported file without implying the editing project is irrelevant. Remove the inference that smooth animation establishes careful editing. Suggested opening: “Review the exported video against the current product, alongside any checks in the editing project. Log each problem with a timestamp and the expected correction. A polished animation does not establish an accurate explanation.”
- **Article 4, line 33:** the requirement combines scene timing and crop, but the Descript cell documents spatial controls only. Split those requirements, or narrow this row to cropping and verify timing separately. The [Scene Editor documentation](https://help.descript.com/visuals/scene-editor-overview) supports position, size, crop and media replacement; it does not establish timing control in that passage.
- **Article 4, line 32:** add a brief distinction between correcting transcript text and changing spoken narration. Keep replacement audio and coordinated caption/timing behavior unverified unless a relevant primary source is added. Do not turn the text-based-editing description into a claim that all spoken corrections happen automatically.
- **Article 5, line 20, optional:** “If it needs ‘and’” is a rough heuristic, not a dependable rule. Prefer “If the takeaway combines two separate jobs, consider splitting it into two videos.”
